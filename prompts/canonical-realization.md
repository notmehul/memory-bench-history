# Canonical realization rules

How fact templates (`<<type | topic=slug | variant>>`) become ledger prose.
These rules exist because blinded raters exploited every asymmetry between
probed facts and distractors that we ever allowed; each rule below closes a
tell that was actually found.

1. **Register parity.** Every sentence is stated plainly and firmly in its
   type's register — decisions as settled decisions, working_rules as
   standing rules, preferences as direct "X prefers..." statements,
   references/outcomes as flat factual notes. No hedging words (maybe,
   might, may, I think, probably) in any variant except
   `near-miss-lower-capacity`, which may hedge lightly (its ledger capacity
   is `speculation`).
2. **Shape parity.** Distractor decisions and working_rules must read as
   BINDING operational policies over governed classes of work (deploys,
   contracts, incidents, access, budgets, reviews) — never artifact trivia
   (where a form lives, what a deck template looks like). Openings vary:
   roughly half team/company-subject, half governed-class-subject — in both
   fact classes.
3. **Length parity.** Distractor canonical word counts are pinned to the
   probed length distribution (scripts/length_pin.py). Counterfactual
   variants use exactly their base sentence's word count.
4. **Numeric parity.** Concrete thresholds/durations/counts appear at
   similar rates in both classes.
5. **Chains and variants.** v1/v2/v3 on one topic are ONE evolving policy
   (same statement shape, changing values). `near-miss-*` variants stay on
   their sibling's topic, perturbed per variant name, with the sibling's
   firmness. `counterfactual-of-*` materially inverts its base.
6. **Uniqueness.** Every sentence unique across the org.

Length target for fresh realizations: 9–13 words.
