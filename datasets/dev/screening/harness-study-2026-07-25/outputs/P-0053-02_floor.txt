# Bug Triage Operating Rhythm

Bug triage runs on a fixed cadence so every issue has a clear next owner, a clear next action, and no ambiguity between sessions.

## Intake and First Response

All bugs enter the shared triage queue as soon as they are reported. The reporter must include a reproducible summary, affected surface area, observed impact, and any logs, screenshots, or links needed to verify the issue.

New bugs are reviewed against the following first-response targets:

- `P0`: immediate acknowledgment, 15 minutes or less
- `P1`: acknowledgment within 1 hour
- `P2`: acknowledgment within 4 business hours
- `P3`: acknowledgment within 2 business days

Acknowledgment means one person has accepted responsibility for the next step. It does not mean the bug is fixed.

## Triage Cadence

The team works the queue in three rhythms:

1. **Continuous intake**
   `P0` and `P1` bugs are handled as they arrive. They do not wait for the next scheduled session.

2. **Twice-daily triage sessions**
   The shared queue is reviewed at `09:00 UTC` and `17:00 UTC` every business day. Each session is 30 minutes. During the session, the triage owner must:
   - confirm severity and priority
   - merge duplicates
   - request missing reproduction details
   - assign an owner for investigation or resolution
   - set the next update time

3. **Weekly backlog sweep**
   Every Wednesday at `16:00 UTC`, the team reviews all open `P2` and `P3` bugs older than 7 days to re-prioritize, close stale items, or escalate blocked work.

## Severity and Escalation

Use these severity levels consistently:

- `P0`: service unavailable, data loss, security exposure, or a broken critical workflow with no workaround
- `P1`: major customer-facing degradation or a broken important workflow with limited workaround
- `P2`: meaningful defect with workaround available or limited blast radius
- `P3`: minor defect, polish issue, or low-frequency edge case

Escalation rules are simple:

- `P0` starts an active response immediately and stays with a live owner until stabilized.
- `P1` must have an investigation owner in the same business day.
- `P2` and `P3` can wait for the next scheduled triage session unless new impact changes the priority.

## How Responsibility Moves Between Sessions

Responsibility must always sit with one named person, never with “the team” or “next shift.”

When a bug leaves a session, the current owner must set all three of these before handing off:

- **Owner**: the single person responsible until the next checkpoint
- **Next action**: the exact next step, written as a verb
- **Next update time**: a specific timestamp, not “tomorrow” or “soon”

Handoffs follow this rule:

- The current owner keeps responsibility until the next owner explicitly accepts it.
- If the next owner has not accepted by the stated update time, responsibility automatically returns to the triage owner for re-assignment.
- If a bug is blocked on another team, the original owner still owns status updates until the receiving team accepts ownership in writing.
- If a session ends with missing information, the triage owner remains the owner until the reporter or investigating engineer supplies what is missing.

## Session Closeout Standard

Every triage session ends with each open bug in one of five states:

- `Assigned`: owner accepted and next update time is set
- `Need Info`: waiting on a named person, with due time set
- `Blocked`: dependency is named, blocker owner is named, and next review time is set
- `Monitoring`: mitigation is in place and the next check is scheduled
- `Closed`: fix verified, duplicate merged, or report invalidated

A bug is not allowed to remain in an unowned or unspecified state between sessions.

## Update Expectations

Open bugs must be updated on this cadence at minimum:

- `P0`: every 30 minutes until stabilized
- `P1`: every 4 hours during business hours
- `P2`: once per business day
- `P3`: once per week

If there is no material progress, the owner still posts a status update stating what is blocked, what was checked, and when the next update will happen.

## Definition of a Clean Queue

The queue is considered healthy when all of the following are true:

- every open bug has exactly one owner
- every open bug has a next update time
- no `P1` bug is unassigned for more than 1 business hour
- no `P2` or `P3` bug is untouched past its required update cadence
- duplicate and invalid reports are closed within 2 business days

This is the operating rhythm to follow by default. If priority changes, update severity first, then reset owner and next update time so responsibility remains explicit.
