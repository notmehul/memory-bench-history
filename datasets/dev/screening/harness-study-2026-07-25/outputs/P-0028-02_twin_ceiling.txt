# Growth Runbook Maintenance Checklist

## Purpose
Keep Growth runbooks accurate, easy to use under time pressure, and ready for routine execution. Formal review is required only after a major incident affecting Growth-owned systems or workflows.

## Ownership
- `Growth Runbook Steward`: one named Growth teammate owns runbook upkeep for the current quarter.
- The steward tracks the `next review due` date for every runbook and reports status in the weekly Growth team meeting.
- Each runbook also has a `runbook owner` responsible for the content of that specific document.

## Refresh Cadence
- Review every active Growth runbook every `90 days`.
- Review any runbook used in a live issue, launch, rollback, or manual recovery within `5 business days` of use.
- Archive or merge any runbook not used and not updated for `12 months`.
- Hold a formal reviewed refresh within `10 business days` after any major incident.

## What Starts an Update
Start the update process immediately when any of the following happens:
- The `90-day` review date is reached.
- A Growth teammate uses the runbook and finds missing, unclear, or outdated steps.
- A launch, tooling change, vendor change, dashboard change, or access change alters the process.
- A KPI definition, alert threshold, naming convention, or escalation path changes.
- A major incident occurs in a Growth-owned workflow or system.

For this checklist, a `major incident` means any incident that:
- blocks a Growth campaign, launch, experiment, or reporting workflow for more than `2 hours`, or
- causes incorrect customer targeting, broken attribution, or missing Growth reporting for more than `1 business day`.

## Required Checks For Each Refresh
- Confirm the runbook still has a clear owner.
- Verify the title matches the actual workflow.
- Check that prerequisites, permissions, links, dashboards, and tools still work.
- Validate every step in order and remove stale branches or duplicate instructions.
- Confirm alert names, thresholds, queries, and expected outputs are current.
- Check rollback, fallback, and escalation instructions.
- Make sure contact points and handoff paths are current.
- Add the `last reviewed` date, reviewer name, and `next review due` date.
- Note whether the refresh was routine or incident-driven.

## Meeting-Ready Review Flow
Use this in the weekly Growth team meeting:
1. Review runbooks due in the next `14 days`.
2. Confirm the owner and steward for each due item.
3. Identify any runbooks touched by launches, tooling changes, or incidents since the last meeting.
4. Assign updates that must be completed before the next meeting.
5. Confirm any major-incident follow-up that requires formal review.
6. Read out overdue items and their new committed completion dates.

## Tracking Standard
Track every runbook in a simple table with:
- `Runbook name`
- `Runbook owner`
- `Last reviewed`
- `Next review due`
- `Status: on track / due soon / overdue`
- `Last trigger: routine / used / change / incident`

The `Growth Runbook Steward` keeps this tracker current and updates it before each weekly team meeting.

## Definition Of Done
A runbook refresh is complete only when:
- the content has been updated,
- links and operational steps have been checked,
- the owner agrees it is usable today,
- the tracker reflects the new `last reviewed` and `next review due` dates, and
- formal signoff is recorded if the refresh followed a major incident.
